Leadership Plan · Term 2026–27

San Vicente Choira year of steadier voices

A working plan for the new term — what we'll build, in what order, and how each of us serves. Prepared by the president for the officers and members.

Our north star

Better music is built on steadier people.

We can't rehearse rich harmony with three random voices on a Saturday. So this year we fix the rhythm and the connection first — and the musical quality we're really after follows from there. We grow into something healthy, not just bigger.

The year in six moves

In order. Each one makes the next one possible.

1

Make June 20 count

Open relationally, not with rules. Ask every member what they want from the choir this year — out loud and in a short anonymous form, since people won't always speak up. Introduce our structure lightly: one clear commitment ask per membership tier.

2

Protect a weekly rehearsal rhythm

Same day, same time, every week — it happens whether or not the director is present. Members learn their parts at home from practice tracks per voice section, so his limited time goes only to what he alone can give: direction and instruments.

3

Appoint section leaders

One reliable singer per voice section (S · A · T · B) to run sectionals and keep their section gently accountable. This shares the load our small officer team can't carry — and ownership is exactly what pulls quieter members back in.

4

Assign service coverage

Stop hoping someone shows up. Pre-assign a minimum committed core for each service across sections. Now recruiting has a purpose: invite targeted to our thinnest sections, not "everyone, somehow."

5

Choose repertoire that scales

Pick pieces that still sound whole with whoever shows — unison or two-part that holds together, not arrangements that collapse without full SATB. Five minutes of warm-ups every practice; record ourselves now and then so we can hear our own progress.

6

Rebuild connection on purpose

Practice-only contact isn't enough. One short, partly-social gathering a month, and a standing place for input where even associate and honorary members are asked their view. Invite our former president and honorary members to be keepers of culture — their memory is an asset.

Quick reference

The decisions, settled — so no one has to wonder.

Membership

Active — commits every service; holds voting rights.

Associate — serves when able; no vote.

Honorary — long-serving; sings when able.

Looking unified

Uniforms are a later, when-funded goal — not this year's spend.

For now: a shared dress code from clothes we already own (e.g. white top, dark bottom). Unified, and free.

Attendance

Recorded quietly on the officer side — members keep using Messenger as before.

The tiers replace scolding: we notice who's drifting so we can reach out with care.

Your three-day prep

A finishing sprint before the June 20 kickoff.

Day 1 — Decide & write

Lock the direction so the rest is execution.

  • Align with reachable officers: officers set the frame, the whole choir gets voice.
  • Finalize the single commitment ask for each tier (active / associate / honorary).
  • Draft the short anonymous "what do you want this year?" form.
  • Choose and lock the protected weekly rehearsal slot.

Day 2 — Build the back office

Set up the systems that run quietly all year.

  • Create the officer-only attendance log (no member login needed).
  • Add a "Songs & Resources" page to the existing choir site.
  • Gather or queue practice tracks per voice section (placeholders are fine for now).
  • Write the June 20 agenda — relational open first, structure second.

Day 3 — Rally the people

Turn structure into a room full of willing members.

  • Confirm officer roles and ask four section leaders to serve.
  • Personally message members about June 20 — a real message, not just a group-chat tag.
  • Prepare your opening words for the meeting.
  • Prepare the shared dress-code suggestion to introduce.

Your authority this year doesn't come from being the oldest member or the best singer. It comes from showing up, bringing order, and serving — and those are already yours.
